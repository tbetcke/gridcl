# ADR-0053: Bullet 3's p = 8 / p = 4 bound is not met by M9h's strategies

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Raised in:** M9h T11b (decision rule, policy and report)
- **Design sections affected:** Roadmap M9h, acceptance bullet 3 (read as ADR-0042 item 1, amended by ADR-0049 and ADR-0052); "Benchmarks" (**Degree sweep (M9h)**); M10 Hardening (benchmarks). Amends ADR-0042, item 1.

## Context

Roadmap M9h, bullet 3: "for quadrilaterals and hexahedra, the time per quadrature point of geometric factors and of a pull-back Newton step at p = 8 is at most 3 times that at p = 4 on every device class (direct evaluation: about 5.8 times)". ADR-0042 item 1 reads it as: factors at most 3; the pull-back's set-up per point at most 3 (ADR-0049: the tensor pull-back's sum-factorised set-up too); a Newton step at most 1.1 · (9/5)^d (3.56 on quadrilaterals, 6.42 on hexahedra); "the ratio is that of the strategy the policy selects at p = 8, timed at p = 8 and at p = 4", at 10^6 and 10^7 points, per class by the geometric mean of the device ratios. ADR-0052 uses 10^6 points on the CPU classes.

T11b's decision rule (`gridcl-bench/src/decide_degree.rs`, which agrees with the independent check `high_order_record.rs::degree_policy_follows_the_decision_rule`) applied to T11a's sweeps gives the policy, and `tensor_cost_ratio_p8_over_p4` then measures bullet 3 on it.

## Problem

71 of 120 ratios exceed their bound (`docs/perf/high-order.md`, "Rule 1", generated by `high_order_report` in the working tree of T11b). Per class and scalar type, the strategy selected at p = 8 and the range of its ratios:

| Class | Type | Quadrilateral factors (bound 3) | Hexahedron factors (bound 3) | Step quad / hex (3.56 / 6.42) | Set-up quad / hex (3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CpuSimd128 | f32 | sumfact 1.43–1.66 | sumfact **3.61–5.65** | sumfact **4.63** / **6.52** | sumfact 0.13 / **16.95** |
| CpuSimd128 | f64 | sumfact 2.17–**4.02** | sumfact 1.67–1.85 | sumfact 2.90 / **7.37** | sumfact 0.76 / **6.74** |
| CpuSimd512 | f32 | sumfact 2.47–**3.54** | sumfact 1.08–1.81 | direct **8.67** / sumfact **8.45** | direct **32.66** / sumfact **4.22** |
| CpuSimd512 | f64 | sumfact 1.34–1.96 | sumfact **3.93–12.25** | direct **7.98** / direct **25.55** | direct **31.90** / direct **35.61** |
| Gpu (10^6 / 10^7) | f32 | shared 1.49–2.07 | sumfact **5.08–13.97** | direct **12.9–19.7** / loops **15.1–35.0** | direct **10.2–10.7** / loops **12.6–20.5** |
| Gpu (10^6 / 10^7) | f64 | shared, sumfact 1.61–**5.16** | sumfact **3.02–44.28** | direct **10.2–17.0** / sumfact **14.6–30.3** | direct **5.6–6.1** / sumfact **5.7–11.0** |

Of the factor ratios 42 of 88 fail, of the pull-back's 29 of 32. The quadrilateral factors pass on most classes; the hexahedron factors fail on four of six (class, type) pairs; the pull-back fails on every class.

The cause is not the operation count. Sum factorisation costs about 1.8 times as much per point at p = 8 as at p = 4, but:

- **GPU factors:** the sum-factorised kernels keep each lane's intermediates, (p + 1)^2 · q values per direction, in registers. On Locust (`m9h-cuda-resources-locust.json`, ptxas), the hexahedron Jacobian kernel at p = 8 has a 20 KB (f32) and 57 KB (f64) stack per thread and spills 41 KB and 144 KB; at p = 4 these are 1.4 KB and 5.4 KB. CUDA hexahedron points with `sumfact`: 0.035 ns per point at p = 4, 0.373 ns at p = 8 (10^7 points).
- **CPU factors:** the same intermediates per lane times the vector size (f64 hexahedron at p = 8: 17.5 KB per lane, eight lanes) leave L1.
- **Pull-back:** the decision rule picks by the 9-iteration total, so at p = 8 it selects `direct` or `loops` where `sumfact` is not 10% faster. `loops`' and `direct`'s set-up multiplies C′ (N × N) with x, (p + 1)^3 work per point on hexahedra: a ratio of 5.8 by construction. The step reads (p + 1)^d coefficients per point from global memory and is memory-bound at p = 8 on GPUs.

The selected strategies are still much faster than M9's kernels at p = 8 (T3's baseline, per point, same device and size): hexahedron J 38–161 times (Locust, Metal, Mac mini, Kathleen), hexahedron points 16–116 times, quadrilateral J and points 2–9 times (Metal's quadrilateral points 0.8 times), the pull-back at 9 iterations 0.9–4.7 times (Locust's hexahedron pull-back has no M9 time at p = 8: `direct` did not compile within 600 s, T3). The p = 4 kernels became fast too, which is what makes the ratio large.

## Options

1. **Keep the design as written: more kernel work in M9h.** New tasks before T11b:
   - a GPU sum-factorised kernel with one work group per cell (or tile) and the per-direction intermediates in shared memory, the layout of libCEED and MFEM, for factors and the tensor pull-back's set-up; the cooperative strategy of T10a did not take the sum-factorised contractions;
   - on CPUs, blocking of the intermediates (fewer lanes per pass at high degree, or contraction order by cache size);
   - a pull-back whose step at p = 8 is within 1.1 · (9/5)^d, and a decision rule that selects it; on GPUs the step is memory-bound, and a strategy with the coefficients of a cell in shared memory would need a cell's points in one work group.
   Then T11a's sweeps again for the changed strategies (about a day of machine time, ADR-0050 to ADR-0052), and T11b. Estimated two to four tasks and the re-sweeps. Even then the Gpu pull-back and the CPU hexahedron factors may not reach the bounds.
2. **Bullet 3 becomes a reported measurement, and M10 takes the bound as a target (proposed).** M9h keeps its strategies and the data-driven policy. `high-order.md` reports, per class, scalar type, cell type and family, the p = 8 / p = 4 ratio of the selected strategy against the bounds of ADR-0042 item 1, and its speedup over M9's `direct` at p = 8 (T3's baseline). M10's benchmark of the final kernels ("geometric factors per second at p = 1, 4 and 8") checks the bound again on the kernels it ships, with the GPU shared-memory sum factorisation of option 1 as its first candidate.
3. **Option 1 on the Gpu class only, option 2 on the CPU classes.** One new task (the GPU shared-memory sum factorisation for factors and the pull-back's set-up), then the Gpu sweeps again (Metal and Locust, about 8–10 h). The Gpu pull-back step stays memory-bound and probably above its bound, so it would need option 2 too.
4. **Bound the speedup over M9 at p = 8 instead of the ratio.** For example, at p = 8 the selected strategy at least as fast as `direct` (T3's baseline). Met by factors on every class except Metal's quadrilateral points (0.8), and by the pull-back only where the policy keeps `direct` (ratio 1 up to noise). It tests a different property from the design's.

## Proposed decision

Option 2. The strategies M9h set out to compare exist, are correct at p = 1–8 on every backend, and the policy chooses among them from data; the cost at p = 8 is far below M9's on the tensor cells. What the bound asks for (a per-point cost nearly independent of p) needs the shared-memory, cell-per-work-group sum factorisation of option 1, which is a kernel design of its own and fits M10's re-benchmark of the final kernels better than a fourth sweep round in M9h. The maintainer may prefer option 3 to keep a GPU result in M9h.

## Consequences

- Text to change in docs/design.md:
  - Roadmap M9h, bullet 3: "for quadrilaterals and hexahedra, the time per quadrature point of geometric factors and of a pull-back Newton step at p = 8 over that at p = 4 is measured and reported per device class against a target of 3 (direct evaluation: about 5.8 times), with the speedup over M9 at p = 8 (ADR-0053)"; the "Acceptance (read as …)" line adds ADR-0053.
  - M10 Hardening, acceptance: add "for quadrilaterals and hexahedra, the time per point of geometric factors and of a pull-back Newton step at p = 8 is at most 3 times that at p = 4 on every device class (ADR-0042 item 1, ADR-0053), or the gap is reported with its cause".
  - "Benchmarks", **Degree sweep (M9h)**: add "`docs/perf/high-order.md` reports the p = 8 / p = 4 ratios of ADR-0042 item 1 and the speedup over M9 at p = 8 (ADR-0053)".
- Public API changes: none.
- Acceptance tests affected: `gridcl-bench/tests/high_order_record.rs::tensor_cost_ratio_p8_over_p4` becomes `tensor_cost_ratio_reported`: every (class, scalar type, cell type, family, part, rule size) of item 1 has a row in the generated report with the selected strategy, the ratio and the bound, and the ratios in the report equal those recomputed from the records. It no longer asserts the bound.
- Plan: M9h's coverage-table row of bullet 3 names this ADR; T11b's note "if a bound fails on a class, stop and draft an ADR" is met by it. Issue #37's bullet 3 gets the same text.
- Later milestones affected: M10 (the bound becomes its target, with option 1's GPU kernel as its first candidate).
